Limerick is one of the easier Irish cities to visit without renting a car.

Most of the places a first-time visitor is likely to see in the city centre are close enough to reach on foot. King John’s Castle, the Medieval Quarter, the Milk Market, the River Shannon and several of Limerick’s best-known traditional pubs can all be combined into a day of walking.

A car becomes more useful if you want to explore County Limerick or rural destinations farther away. For Limerick city itself, however, it is usually unnecessary.

This guide combines local experience in Limerick with current information from official transport, tourism and attraction sources.

## Do you need a car in Limerick?

For a normal city break, no.

Limerick City and County Council itself describes the city as an ideal place to explore on foot, and that matches the practical reality of staying in the centre.

The historic centre is compact enough that walking should normally be your first option rather than trying to find a bus for every journey.

Colbert Station, O'Connell Street, the riverside and the Medieval Quarter are all within a relatively compact central area. King John’s Castle is also within walking distance of the train and bus station.

For destinations outside the centre, including the University of Limerick and Plassey Beach, public transport becomes more useful.

## Getting from Shannon Airport to Limerick without a car

Shannon Airport is the main airport for Limerick and reaching the city without a rental car is straightforward.

Bus Éireann Route 343 connects Shannon Airport with Limerick and Colbert Station. The service operates 24 hours a day, seven days a week, with buses running as frequently as every 20 minutes during weekday peak periods and every 30 minutes at many other times. Overnight services continue at a lower frequency.

Based on local experience, the journey typically feels straightforward and there are several stops in Limerick before or around the main station. Allow roughly an hour rather than planning a tight connection, since journey time can vary with traffic and the number of stops.

For most independent travellers, the 343 makes renting a car at Shannon Airport unnecessary if Limerick is the first destination.

A taxi or pre-booked transfer is more convenient if you have a lot of luggage, are travelling as a group or simply want door-to-door transport. Private transfers between Shannon Airport and Limerick are also commercially available.

## Getting around Limerick on foot

Walking is the easiest way to explore central Limerick.

It also makes more sense than repeatedly waiting for buses when several attractions are relatively close to each other.

A useful first walk is along the River Shannon and through the Medieval Quarter. The river is not simply a boundary around the city. It is one of the defining parts of Limerick and gives you some of the best views of its historic buildings.

![Riverside promenade along the River Shannon in central Limerick](/images/articles/limerick-without-a-car/shannon-river.webp)

There are several official walking routes around the city, including riverside trails and the Three Bridges area.

Wear comfortable shoes and keep one thing in mind: the weather can change quickly.

A morning that looks dry does not guarantee a dry afternoon. Check the forecast before leaving, but plan on the possibility that conditions will change while you are outside.

## King John's Castle is the main historical stop

If you only pay for one historical attraction in Limerick, King John’s Castle is the strongest candidate.

![Exterior view of King John's Castle beside the River Shannon in Limerick](/images/articles/limerick-without-a-car/king-johns-castle.webp)

The castle stands on King’s Island beside the Shannon and covers more than 800 years of local history. The visitor experience combines the surviving castle with exhibitions, multimedia displays, reconstructed spaces and views across Limerick.

Inside, you can see archaeological remains beneath the later castle, recreated medieval environments such as the blacksmith’s area and military spaces, and videos depicting characters and events connected with the castle’s history and sieges.

The towers are one of the highlights. From the upper sections of the castle you get wide views across the River Shannon and Limerick city.

The official recommendation is to allow around 90 minutes. If you enjoy history and want to read the exhibits rather than rush through them, allowing two to three hours is more realistic.

As of August 2026, an adult ticket is listed at €16.50. Student, senior, child and family tickets are also available.

The castle is especially worthwhile if medieval history interests you. Even visitors whose interest comes more from medieval fantasy, castles or series such as Game of Thrones may find the reconstructed spaces more engaging than a conventional museum.

## Visit the Milk Market at the right time

The Milk Market has operated in Limerick since 1852 and remains one of the city’s best-known places for local food and produce.

Timing matters.

Saturday is the most useful day to include it in an itinerary because that is when the main farmers market operates, with local produce, artisan foods, food to go and other stalls.

Current published hours are 11:00 to 15:00 on Friday, 8:00 to 15:00 on Saturday and 11:00 to 15:00 on Sunday.

If your trip includes a Saturday, visiting the Milk Market in the morning before heading toward the river and Medieval Quarter creates a natural walking route through the city.

## Where to experience a traditional pub in Limerick

Limerick is worth experiencing after the museums and attractions close.

![Traditional live music session inside a pub in Limerick](/images/articles/limerick-without-a-car/traditional-pub.webp)

Dolan’s is particularly good for travellers interested in traditional Irish music rather than simply looking for somewhere to drink.

It is a traditional pub and live music venue on Dock Road. Its current schedule lists free traditional Irish music sessions at 21:00 from Wednesday through Sunday.

From local experience, the atmosphere during these sessions is friendly rather than formal. You are likely to find both Irish customers and people from other countries, and the musicians often interact with the audience.

The Locke is another easy option for a first visit to Limerick. It sits beside the river in the Medieval Quarter and combines outdoor seating with traditional Irish music and dancing. Its current programme advertises traditional music and dance seven nights a week.

JJ Bowles offers a somewhat different experience. It is widely regarded as Limerick’s oldest pub, dating to 1794, and its riverside beer garden has views toward King John’s Castle.

If you want only one pub, choose according to what interests you most: Dolan’s for the music session, The Locke for a lively riverside evening with music and dancing, or JJ Bowles for an older traditional pub atmosphere.

## Plassey Beach and the UL riverside

For a quieter side of Limerick, head toward the University of Limerick.

The university is around 4 km outside the city centre and occupies a large parkland campus beside the River Shannon.

The riverside area commonly called UL Beach is also referred to as Plassey Beach. It is less a conventional tourist attraction and more a place to escape the movement of the centre, sit beside the river and enjoy the surrounding landscape.

![Plassey Beach and riverside scenery near the University of Limerick](/images/articles/limerick-without-a-car/plassey-beach.webp)

On a good-weather day, it can be one of the more relaxing places to spend part of a morning.

The wider riverside route is particularly interesting. An official 3.25 km walking and cycling route links the city-centre side of the Shannon with the University of Limerick, and historical documentation for the area identifies the medieval remains of Castletroy Castle along the extended riverside route.

If you have only one day in Limerick, this is optional rather than essential. King John’s Castle and the historic centre should take priority.

If the weather is good and you prefer nature, however, a riverside morning followed by the castle in the afternoon and traditional music in the evening makes a very different Limerick itinerary.

## Using buses in Limerick

You probably will not need a bus for most central sightseeing.

They become useful for places such as the University of Limerick, Castletroy and other areas outside the compact centre.

Bus Éireann accepts TFI Leap Cards, TFI Go mobile tickets and cash. Cash tickets can be purchased from the driver, and TFI recommends having exact change when possible. Leap fares are generally cheaper than equivalent cash fares.

For current departures, use the TFI journey planner or live departure information rather than relying on an old timetable.

Do not assume that every Limerick bus runs late at night. Many city services finish or become much less useful late in the evening.

The major exception for visitors is Route 343 to Shannon Airport, which runs around the clock.

If you stay in a pub after local buses have finished, a regular taxi is the simplest alternative.

## Where to stay in Limerick without a car

Location matters more than having access to parking.

Look for accommodation in or immediately around the city centre, particularly near the River Shannon, O'Connell Street, the Medieval Quarter or the central station area.

Clayton Hotel Limerick and Limerick Strand Hotel are examples of riverfront hotels in central locations, but there are also less expensive accommodation options.

The important point is not the hotel brand. It is avoiding accommodation far outside the centre if you plan to depend on walking.

A central hotel lets you explore during the day, return to your room and go back out for dinner or music without having to organise transport every time.

## Is Limerick safe to explore on foot?

Central Limerick generally feels straightforward to explore on foot, including during the evening.

As in any city, normal precautions still apply. Keep track of your belongings, pay attention to your surroundings late at night and use a taxi if you are returning somewhere farther from the centre after public transport has finished.

There is little reason for a normal visitor to avoid walking around the main central tourist areas simply because they do not have a car.

## A simple one-day Limerick itinerary without a car

If the weather is good, spend the morning around the Shannon or take a bus toward the University of Limerick and the Plassey riverside area.

Return to the centre for lunch and spend the main part of the afternoon at King John’s Castle. Allow enough time to see the exhibitions and climb the castle rather than rushing through it.

Afterwards, walk through the Medieval Quarter and along the river.

Finish the day at a traditional pub. Depending on the night and the atmosphere you want, Dolan’s, The Locke or JJ Bowles can all fit naturally into a car-free itinerary.

If you are visiting on Saturday, move the Milk Market to the beginning of the day.

If the weather is poor, skip the trip toward UL and concentrate on the compact city centre.

## Is Limerick worth visiting without a car?

Yes.

In fact, having a car adds relatively little to a short visit focused on Limerick city.

Walking works well for the historic centre, public transport connects the city with the University of Limerick and Shannon Airport, and taxis solve the occasional late-night journey.

The main limitation appears when you want to use Limerick as a base for exploring rural Ireland. Reaching smaller villages and remote attractions can require more planning.

But for Limerick itself, arriving without a car is not a compromise. For many visitors, it is the simpler way to experience the city.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you visit Limerick without renting a car?

Yes. Most major central sights can be reached on foot, while buses cover areas farther from the centre.

### Can I get from Shannon Airport to Limerick by public transport?

Yes. Route 343 connects Shannon Airport and Limerick and currently operates 24 hours a day.

### Is Limerick walkable?

Yes. The city’s official tourism information specifically recommends exploring Limerick on foot, and the central sightseeing area is relatively compact.

### How long should I spend at King John's Castle?

The castle recommends approximately 1.5 hours. Travellers who enjoy history may prefer to allow two to three hours.

### Can I pay cash on Limerick buses?

Yes. Bus Éireann accepts cash on board, as well as Leap Card and eligible TFI Go tickets.

### What is the best day to visit the Milk Market?

Saturday offers the main farmers market and the widest range of food and produce stalls.
